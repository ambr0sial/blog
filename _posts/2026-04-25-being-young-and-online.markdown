i was nine when i made my first batch virus. nine!! i didnt even fully understand what i was doing, i just knew it was something i wasnt supposed to do and that felt like enough reason

i found my way into certain communities early and stayed there for years. the kind that run on discord servers and private forums, where you need someone to vouch for you to get further in. those communities had a specific culture. everything was about who has more, who does more, who's more impressive. people would flex tools, flex money, flex hits on websites. oh you got a corporate site?? cool, someone else just defaced a government one. constant one-upmanship. doxxing was just normal there, something you did when you needed leverage or when someone annoyed you enough. i saw it happen constantly and at some point stopped thinking twice about it

i was in it. not just watching, actually in it

what kept me there was the thrill. not even really the skill at first, just the feeling of doing things that most people dont do and being in spaces most people dont know exist. i genuinely didnt understand how much that was getting to me at twelve or thirteen

whats weird is that almost ten years later some of that is still there. like i'll be talking to someone and part of my brain goes "what if i ratted them" and theres this little dopamine hit that comes with just the thought. i would never actually do it, not to anyone i care about, but the thought still shows up. i think spending years in those spaces changed how i think about people a little, and im not sure that fully goes away. i dont see everyone as a target its just that the thought is available in a way it probably wouldnt be if i'd grown up somewhere else online ig

the shame i feel about that era is i think kinda about how okay everything felt at the time, like being around that kind of stuff long enough stops reading as wrong. and then at some point you leave the com, and your sense of normal shifts back and you look at the old version of yourself and go oh. oh that was bad actually

i still do malware. that part never went away. for example, since i talked about it in another post, wannacry was a turning point for me in how i thought about it, because it made it feel real and serious in a way that a script you run in a vm doesnt. hospitals freezing, banks going down, actual people affected. i was fascinated and kind of horrified at the same time. i still find it genuinely interesting, its probably what i want to do long term, but it stopped feeling like a game after that

im almost nineteen. im still figuring out how much of who i was in those communities is still me versus how much has actually changed. honestly probably both
